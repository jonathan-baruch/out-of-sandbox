Day 4: Cerro Castillo

A much needed sleep was needed after the long drive from Patagonia National Park the night before. After making myself some breakfast and enjoying my last views of Rio Tranquillo, which I had come to consider a perfect landing spot between adventures over the last few days, it was time to say goodbye.

Own my way out, I decided to check-in with two hitchhikers by the side of the road. They turned out to be a newly wed couple on their year-long honeymoon travesing the globe looking for the best mountaineering climbs. In this moment, I didn’t realize how significant these two would be to my life over the next few days. They happened to also be heading to Cerro Castillo so I thought it a perfect chance to have some company along the way and so glad that I did.

*** Hitchhiking in Chile *** Unlike the concerns in the United States, hitchiking in Chile is as common as taking the bus. It is an acceptable way to get around and has built its own sub-culture around manners and communication.

Before starting the day, we grabbed some lunch at a restaurant (Ruta 7 Sur) that overlooks the mountain views that we would soon spend the next three days in. For lunch, we tried their famous Ruta 7 Sur sandwich, a massive bun layered with mayo, ketchup, bacon, egg, beef, lettuce, tomato, cheese, onion, and pickled cabbage. It was, honestly, an incredible meal leaving me with a happy and full stomach as a last reminder before the mountains.

More about these two new friends later.

When we finally arrived at the start of the trail, we were told we had missed timing to enter the mountain and that they did not recommend ascending that day. Thought the skies looked blue, it was the last good day of the week and the rain clouds would start rolling in for the week. Weather in the mountains can be very dynamic and open and close opportunities within a few hours. To not put ourselves at risk, we decided to setup camp at the base camp and see if tomorrow would be any better.

Luckily, the entraces of the park have huts where rangers can live in while they work the area. They have running water, a bathroom, and a fire pit to stay warm. Since the weather was so good, we decided to take it slow today and enjoy the beautiful views of the mountain and light ourselves a fire for the night.

While making dinner, we talked all about their adventures through Thailand, through the huts in Nepal in the snow, through Agentina, around the South of Chile, in the mountains for Georgia, and the future trip to Colorado.

Michal and Karina are a newly wed couple from the city of Gdansk in Poland. Gdansk is a major seaport along on the Baltic. While on a expedition in Georgia, where treks have largely only been mapped by Russian military and other mountaineers on database websites, Michael proposed to Karina and named the trail Love Pass.

Fun Fact: Gdansk is the hometown of the founder of the Fahrenheit system. The reason that the scale is changed from Celcius is becuase 0º is the coldest temperature measured in Gdansk and 100º was a wrongful measurement of the temperature of the human body.

Michal studied Computer Science and Finance in university. In his early 20s, he and some friends attempted a start-up that would translate sign-language, but eventually they needed to fofeit the idea when it became too technically challenging to account for all of short-hand and context-dependent language used amoung sign-language speakers. Today, he works in the energy brokerage sector. After learning mountaineering from his father, he spent most of his life mountaineering, climbing, casually running an 80km race, cycling through Germany, or learning the guitar. He also has a sister studying in Bostom and other cousins across the states like Texas and Colorado.

Karina was born in the country-side and is studying to be a doctor, with hopes to become a cardiologist. She has also been climbing for years across her own adventures and has probably mastered the art of mountain cooking with a mean bowl of rice, powdered milk, and sugar. Though extremely humble about it, when she trains for them, she has consistently won 5k to 20k races. Closing each evening and starting each morning with a cup of tea was such a perfect way to keep warm and bring some comfort out here.

It was fascnating to discuss their thoughts on mountain safety and mountaineering, a topic I’ve always been interested in but has always felt a bit out of reach. We talked about the dangers of 1% risks, the effects of compounding (even small and indpendent) bad luck could lead to bad or rushed decisions that could lead to risky situations, how to manage pack weight, and how to weigh between abstract fears and the real risks in the moment.

The two of them plan to start having a child when they return to Poland. It was so heart-warming to hear how ready Michal is to start being a father and join their two lives together.

Among other topics, we spent the night discussing global politics around the world, the existential threat of Russia, politics in the EU, how we viewed relationships and communication, our place amoung the stars, and the amazing experiences we both had among our travels. It was an seredipitous and special way to feel at home, on a Shabbat evening, in Chile.

We eventually went to bed at nearly 2am as the fire trickled down to embers and the milky way had traveled across the sky.

I had never seen such clear skies before and the milky way so bright. Something that I didn’t remember that I would see is that even all of the constellations are flipped by 180º and that the Winter constellations to the northern hemisphere and the summer constellations to the Southern hemisphere.

When the landscape and mountains feel big, it can always be dwarfed by our place in the universe and each star as its own sun, and our blue dot floating among them.
