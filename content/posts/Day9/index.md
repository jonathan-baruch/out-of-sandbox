Day 1 in Futaleufu would be for learning to fly fishing. I had first fly fished many years ago and fell in love with the tranquility and skill that it took. Futaleufu is regarded as a world-class desintation for fly fishing that is primarily for trout. One of my goals on this trip was to pick up some new hobbies and experiences, so there was no better way to learn to fish than from here.

My guide's name was Poncho. This seemed to be his boat and gear that he runs out of the expeditions company Bochinche Expeditions.

The morning would just be me and Poncho on the river. After showing me some casting techniques and gave time to practice on the shore, he would do most of the hard work at paddling against the current to position me in various places along the river to cast my line.

The conditions wavered between dumping rain and calm. And yes, my boots and pants were soaked after the experience, but when the rain would pass, it would be totally peace - listing to the sound of the river, looking for the trout to jump up and catch bugs, and feeling the line in my hands as the mountains would open up around us. I would still say that it was a perfect morning to fish.

Poncho changed out the fly a number of times as we tested out the fish. We needed to alter between feeding zones. Trout can hang out by the shallow waters near the plants where bugs would often be, so we would need a line and fly that wouldn't sync as much. In the deeper waters where bio-material would create bubbles and foam as it floated down and created eddies, we needed a heavy wire and a fly that would sink deeper in the water. The goal was to cast the fly into the area you though fish would be, then keep the fly in the water as long as possible by releasing more line, then pulling the line back with your hands to keep the fly moving along he surface or at the depth you want.

Unfotunately, the one fish that snagged ended up escaping and all I caught was a bit of the plants. Still, I was excited to learn and had a great time on the river. I can definitely imagine continuing to learn to fish in the future.

I spent the rest of the day relaxing at the hostel, chatting with the other guests, talking about their own fishing experiences and backpacking knowledge. It's been a very busy and active hostel with lots of guests coming and going.

Futaleufu since near the border with Argentina, so many people pass through this town on their way North to Puerto Montt, heading South along the Careterra Austral, or out to Santiago or Argentina to either continue their adventures or escape the rain by heading to warmer climates.

There's something kind of fun about timing the weather and letting nature dectate how the plans will unfold.
