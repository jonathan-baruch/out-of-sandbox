Puyuhuapi is a small, picturesque village that sits on the edge of the Puyuhuapi Fjord. It's surrounded by dense forests and steep mountains, giving it a secluded, almost fairytale-like atmosphere.

The city was founded in the 1930s by German settlers, which is reflected in the street names like Hamburgo, the wooden, alpine style architecture, and the local culture.

The name "Puyuhuapi" comes from an indigenous word meaning "place of fog" due to the area's misty climate. When we drove in, we immediately got this sense, and not just because the summer season had finally signaled its end.

The surrounding forests are noticeably thick and almost like a rainforest. Chile really has a way of keeping you on your toes as you traverse through various lattitudes and climates. This was distinctively different than the granite mountains and low grasslands that made up most of the South.

Now that the rain has started, it is expected to continue throughout the next two weeks with small windows of peace between downpours. This made for the town being very quiet with everyone staying indoors.

For us, this was a perfect day to recover our legs form Cerro Castillo and refresh our clothes and equipment. Michal and I set out to find a laundromat and were initally recommended to go to someone's house. That house said that they weren't available and to try next door. Again, that house told us to go next door. After two more tries, we finally knocked on someone's front door and they took us to a side door that had a table for a reception and a washing machine. We were charged ~$4/kilo, but this was surprisingly cheaper than what you can get charged in other towns. They noted us down in their book and said that they would message us on WhatsApp once the clothes were done.

When they were picked up, the clothes had been dried over their living room's wooden stove (like most houses in Chile have down here), and returned all of the clothes folded in a bag. For $4/kilo, this was the best laundry service that I've ever had.

Finally, my gear and clothes were ready for the next half of this journey.

That night, we drank tea and shared cookies while discussing the field of medicine in Poland and the travels ahead.

On the news playing in the back by the owners of the house we were staying was discussing the news from around the world.

We set out to get up early the next morning for breakfast and try our chances at a clear morning.
